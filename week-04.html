The Issued Map

You do not start with the territory. You start with the map that was issued. Reconstruction treats that map as an act of publication — a frame someone printed and called complete — not as the architecture itself. Once the issued map is the only navigable surface, unprinted rooms stop counting as real. People argue inside the legend and never ask who held the press. The structure underneath did not disappear when the map hardened. It stopped being treated as addressable. What you call “how the world works” is often the success of that printing run, not a survey of the ground. You can spend years proving yourself against the legend and still never touch the rooms the sheet left blank on purpose. Reconstruction does not hand you a second tourist brochure. It returns attention to the blank margins the official sheet trained you to ignore.

The official story needs the map to feel natural. An issued map implies an issuer, a date of lock, a list of what was left off. Naming the issuance would reopen who chose the legend and who lost speech when the blank was sold as empty ground. So the sheet is taught as the world, and the margin is trained out of language. Amnesia that looks like common sense is cheaper than admitting the print run was a decision.

Which map did you inherit as “just how it is” — and what went unspeakable the day you stopped questioning who printed it?

— Bjorn Aeolus
