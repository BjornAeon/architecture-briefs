The Borrowed Beginning

Every account has to start somewhere, and the starting point it picks decides what can be asked afterward. Reconstruction treats a beginning as a joint, not a foundation. A joint is where two pieces were fitted together, and the fit is usually clean enough that you read past it. When a story opens with a confident first scene, ask what had to be carried in from outside that scene to make it stand. Names, tools, rules, and a sense of who counts as an authority all arrive already finished. Nobody explains where they came from, because the story needs them in place before it can begin. The beginning is borrowed. What it borrowed from is the part worth recovering, and it is usually sitting one step behind the first line, unmarked. You can test it on any origin you were handed. Find the first thing the story takes for granted, then ask who supplied it and when. The answer is rarely inside the story. That gap is the joint, and it is where the structure shows.

The official story needs a clean start because a clean start closes the question of what came before it. Admitting the opening was borrowed would mean admitting the account is a middle that was given a first page. So the borrowed material gets called background, and background gets called settled.

Where did you accept someone else's first page as the start of your own story?
